# v3.3 layout findings

Investigation notes from the 2026-08-23 session, when the LED and U9 footprints were updated on
`poly_kybd_split72_left` and DRC was run against a regenerated copper pour for the first time in a
long while. Four findings, in descending order of how much they matter. §2 — the 88-violation
refill mystery that dominated the session — is **solved and confirmed on hardware**.

Everything below was measured directly from the `.kicad_pcb` files with scripted geometry
(rectangle-to-segment and point-in-polygon against the stored `filled_polygon` data), not read off
the canvas.

---

## 1. The USB-C shield is not connected to GND — 32 DRC violations, pre-existing

`USB1` and `USB2` pin **SHIELD** is connected to nothing in the schematic. KiCad therefore
auto-names its net:

```
net 371  "Net-(USB1-SHIELD)"
net 369  "Net-(USB2-SHIELD)"
```

An auto-generated `Net-(...)` name **is** the diagnosis — it means no wire, no label, no power symbol
reaches that pin.

Each connector has four `thru_hole` shield legs (`USB1.13`, `USB2.13`), and they sit in plated holes
surrounded by the GND pour on every copper layer. Measured overlap of the pour into the shield pads:

| layer | shield pads | overlapping | worst gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| F.Cu | 8 | 8 | −0.350 mm |
| In1.Cu | 8 | 8 | −0.350 mm |
| In2.Cu | 8 | 8 | −0.350 mm |
| B.Cu | 8 | 8 | −0.349 mm |

**8 pads × 4 copper layers = 32 clearance violations**, plus the matching hole-clearance errors.

⚠️ **This is entirely pre-existing** — identical to three decimals before and after the 2026-08-23
refill, and present in every revision going back. It has never mattered functionally, because the
legs are physically soldered into holes ringed with GND copper, so **the shield has always been
grounded in reality**. The schematic simply never said so, which is why v3.2 boards work while DRC
objects.

**Fix:** tie `SHIELD` to **GND** in `rp_pico.kicad_sch`. The pour then legitimately connects to those
legs instead of colliding with them, and all 32 disappear. This is also the electrically correct
thing to do.

### 1b. `SW1` rotary encoder — 8 more, cosmetic

`SW1` (`ENC_A`, `ENC_B` and an unnetted hole) overlaps the pour by −0.52 mm, ×4 layers = 8 errors.
**`SW1` is marked DNP** and is not fitted, so these are cosmetic. A DRC exclusion is more appropriate
than a layout change.

## 2. ✅ FIXED by redrawing the GND pour — but the MECHANISM is not established

**What works:** redrawing the priority-2 board-wide GND pour cleared all 88 violations
(confirmed on hardware, 2026-08-24). That is the fix. Everything below is about *why*, and
the honest answer is that we do not know.

⚠️ **An earlier version of this section claimed the cause was a self-intersecting outline.
That claim does not survive the evidence and has been withdrawn.** The reasoning was that
the old outline stored vertices 4 and 19 as the identical point `(199.400, 56.700)` with an
excursion spliced in between them. That is real, but so is the same defect in the
**working** replacement: the new 35-vertex pour still has a duplicate vertex
(v15 ≡ v34 at `(199.39, 56.6928)`) and still has **edge 14 and edge 34 collinear and
overlapping for 6.96 mm** in opposite directions — a zero-width sliver of exactly the same
class. A board carrying that degeneracy now passes DRC, so the degeneracy alone is not
sufficient to cause the violations.

⚠️ Two further corrections to that withdrawn analysis, both worth keeping:

- **There was never a proper self-intersection, in either outline.** The detector used a
  plain `(d1>0) != (d2>0)` sign test, which treats a **zero** cross product — a vertex lying
  exactly *on* another edge — as the negative side, so a T-junction reads as a crossing.
  What is actually present is the collinear overlap above.
- **Island removal and zone priority are ruled out**, tested directly by the author: every
  `island_removal_mode` / `island_area_min` setting, and changed priorities, all with no
  effect on the violations.

### What actually changed between the failing and working pours

| | failing (prio 2) | working (prio 4) |
|---|---|---|
| outline vertices | 30 | 35 (redrawn) |
| bbox | x 22.4–212.0, y 25.6–159.9 | x 21.9–213.8, y 27.0–159.3 |
| clearance | 0.200 | 0.200 |
| thermal gap / spoke | 0.5 | 0.508 |
| island removal | mode 1, 10 mm² | off |
| filled_polygon count | 795 | 317 |
| duplicate vertex | yes | **yes (still)** |
| collinear overlapping edges | yes | **yes (still, 6.96 mm)** |

With island removal and priority excluded, what remains is the **outline geometry itself**
(redrawn, and filling into 317 fragments instead of 795) and the thermal gap. Which of those
mattered is untested, and testing it would mean deliberately reintroducing a broken pour.

### The diagnostic signature — still the useful part

⚠️ **The saved fill was correct and DRC still reported zero.** Taking a via KiCad named as
`actual 0,0000 mm`:

```
via D-C @(132.3115, 93.68275)   size 0.6096   drill 0.3048
  F.Cu / In1.Cu / In2.Cu / B.Cu    copper gap +0.2005 mm    hole gap +0.3529 mm
```

Board-wide, **744 of 746** foreign vias sat at exactly **+0.200 mm** — the zone's local
clearance — with `filled_areas_thickness no`, so those polygons *are* the copper edge. That
contradiction is the thing to recognise: **when the file says one number and DRC says
another, DRC is measuring a fill it computed in memory, not the one on disk.** Redrawing the
zone outline is the cheap first move; chasing fill settings is not.

Two operator-visible symptoms that misled the investigation for a long time:

- **Assigning the zone no net or VSYS "fixed" it.** Not a clue about nets — a different net
  simply changes which regions fill.
- **It looked confined to one key row.** Nothing about that row was unusual; density there
  was ordinary (33 tight via pairs, against 28/37/36/22 in the others). The violations were
  at y 89.8–97.0 while the suspicious outline corner is at y 56.7–67.6.

### Measured from the files, all zero

| Hypothesis | Result |
|---|---|
| Foreign vias touched by the fill | 0 of 1345, all 4 layers |
| Via ↔ foreign track overlap, at 0 mm and at 0.15 mm | 0 |
| Vias with no net | 0 |
| Free-via net conflicts ([KiCad #13516](https://gitlab.com/kicad/code/kicad/-/work_items/13516)) | 0 (246 free vias) |
| GND association dropped on vias with >2 tracks | only 3 GND vias qualify |
| Foreign vias with no knockout at all | 0 |
| GND vias crowding foreign vias closer than 2× clearance | 9, all ≥ +0.196 mm |
| Same-priority zones overlapping | 2 at prio 0, both GND (same net) |
| Custom DRC rules, DRC exclusions, netclass patterns | none / 0 / none |
| KiCad version skew | none — 9.0 before and after |

The right board needed nothing; its pour is a simple 11-vertex polygon.

## 3. Zone structure is accreted, and the two boards disagree

| | left | right |
|---|---|---|
| GND zones | 5 | 5 |
| priorities | 0, 0, 1, 2, 3 | **0, 0, 0, 0, 0** |
| layers | all span `F.Cu B.Cu In1.Cu In2.Cu` | same |
| local clearance | 0.2 ×4, **0.254** ×1 | 0.2 ×4, **0.254** ×1 |
| thermal gap / spoke | 0.5 ×3, **0.508** ×2 | **0.508** ×5 |
| island removal | on the prio-2 pour only | none |

On the left, one board-wide pour (prio 2, 189.5 × 134.2 mm) with four small zones (7–19 mm) stacked
**inside** it in the MCU corner, three of them at *lower* priority so they are largely suppressed. Two
of those carry older, looser settings (0.508 thermal gap, one at 0.254 clearance).

On the right, **all five sit at priority 0** — same net, so not a short, but the fill result is
order-dependent rather than deliberate.

None of this creates a short by itself (every clearance is ≥ 0.2 mm against a 0.15 mm requirement),
but it makes refills unpredictable, and it is the accretion that let §2's malformed outline sit
unnoticed — five stacked pours with mixed priorities and mixed settings is not a shape anyone
reviews. Worth consolidating, or at least making the priorities and settings deliberate and
identical across the two boards.

## 4. `U9` exposed pad is now unconnected

The 2026-08-23 schematic edit replaced a `hierarchical_label "GND"` at `(76.2, 249.555)` in
`rp_pico.kicad_sch` with a `no_connect`, adding one net:

```
nets 439 → 440      new: unconnected-(U9-EP-Pad9)
```

So U9's exposed pad went from **tied to GND** to **floating**. Boya's datasheet §9.5 Note 1 calls the
pad floating, so this is defensible — but it was not the stated intent at the time, and it changes
what the pour does around U9. Worth being deliberate about rather than inheriting from an ERC fix.

U9's eight signal pads verify **8/8** against the existing nets, so the LGA-8 footprint swap is
electrically sound:

```
pad 1 QSPI_SS   pad 2 QSPI_SD1  pad 3 QSPI_SD2  pad 4 GND
pad 5 QSPI_SD0  pad 6 QSPI_SCLK pad 7 QSPI_SD3  pad 8 VDD
```

## Method notes

- ⚠️ **KiCad does not persist DRC markers to disk.** There is no marker data in a `.kicad_pcb` — a
  session spent looking for it. To share DRC results, use **DRC dialog → Save Report to File…** and
  commit the `.rpt`. That report is what finally located this problem; every prior theory was built
  on inference.
- ⚠️ **Row numbering is 0-indexed in conversation** but 1-indexed if you cluster LED y-coordinates.
  Confirm which before matching a report to a row; a one-row offset sent this investigation at the
  wrong band for several rounds.
- **Measure both tails.** A via with *no* knockout shows a **large** distance to the nearest fill
  edge, not a small one. Only checking the minimum will miss it entirely.
- **Pair the right rule with the right item.** Plated through-hole pads are copper-to-copper
  (`min_clearance` 0.15); non-plated holes are hole-to-copper (`min_hole_clearance` 0.13). Applying
  0.15 to both inflated the count from 10 to 123.
- **`filled_areas_thickness no`** means the stored polygons are the real copper edge. If it were
  absent (legacy), the polygons would be stroked by `min_thickness` and every measured gap would need
  correcting by half that.

Board settings for reference: one netclass (`Default`, clearance 0.15), `min_clearance` 0.15,
`min_hole_clearance` 0.13, `min_resolved_spokes` 2, no `.kicad_dru`, no DRC exclusions.
